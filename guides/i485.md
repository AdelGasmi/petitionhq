# Drafting Guide — I-485 Adjustment of Status

The I-485 is a status adjustment — not a new petition arguing merit. USCIS is checking that the underlying immigrant petition is approved or approvable, a visa number is available, the applicant is admissible, and the application is properly supported with documents. Letters here are **administrative and personal support documents**, not legal briefs.

**Key facts as of 2025–2026:**
- **Fee change (Oct 28, 2025):** USCIS no longer accepts paper checks or money orders. All fees paid online via pay.gov / USCIS Pay. Always include the payment confirmation number in cover letters and document lists.
- **Public charge (Form I-944):** No longer required following the 2022 rule rescinding the 2019 public charge rule. Do not reference or include it.
- **I-693 validity:** Medical exam is valid for 2 years from the civil surgeon's signature date. Flag if the exam is approaching expiration.
- **Biometrics:** Applicants aged 14–79 must pay the $85 biometrics fee. Include in the fee summary.
- **Priority date vs. filing date:** For EB applicants from backlogged countries (China, India, Philippines, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras), confirm whether the applicant is using Chart A (Final Action Date) or Chart B (Dates for Filing). State explicitly in the cover letter.

## cover-letter — I-485 Cover Letter

### Purpose

A navigational document for the USCIS officer. It introduces the filing, identifies the applicant and their basis for adjustment, provides all key reference numbers, and lists every document in the package. It is not a legal argument — that was made in the underlying I-140 or other petition. A cover letter that accidentally makes legal arguments, hedges on facts, or omits reference numbers creates delays.

### Required Structure

**Header block** (before salutation):
```
[Date — month spelled out]

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services
[Correct lockbox address for applicant's state — do not guess; flag as ACTION REQUIRED if unknown]

Re: Form I-485, Application to Adjust Status
    Applicant: [Full Legal Name]
    Date of Birth: [Month DD, YYYY]
    Country of Birth: [Country]
    I-140 Receipt No.: [IOE-XXXXXXXXXX] / Approval Date: [Date] (or "Pending")
    A Number: [if previously assigned; otherwise omit]
    Priority Date: [if relevant to visa availability]
```

**Para 1 — Applicant identification and basis**
State who the applicant is, their current immigration status and authorized stay, and the exact basis for adjustment. For NIW/EB-2: "Dr. Sarah Lin Chen is a national of the People's Republic of China currently present in the United States in valid H-1B status, authorized to remain through [Date] pursuant to her I-94. Dr. Chen files this I-485 based on her I-140 immigrant petition for EB-2 classification as a professional holding an advanced degree with a National Interest Waiver, approved by USCIS on [Date] (Receipt No. [IOE-XXX])."

Do not say the applicant is "seeking" a green card — say they are filing the I-485 based on the approved/pending petition.

**Para 2 — Visa number availability**
State why a visa number is currently available (immediate availability for EB-1/EB-2 or based on the current Visa Bulletin chart A vs. chart B filing). If concurrent filing with a pending I-140, state that explicitly. Do not omit this — USCIS will note the absence.

**Para 3 — Dependents (if applicable)**
"Dr. Chen's spouse, [Name], and minor child, [Name] (date of birth: [Date]), are filing concurrent I-485 applications as derivative beneficiaries. Their applications are included in this package." List each derivative separately.

**Document list — the most important part**
Number every item. Be exhaustive. USCIS will check this list against the package. Missing items will trigger an RFE or rejection.

Organize as:
```
Principal Applicant — [Full Name]
1. Form I-485, signed and dated [Date]
2. Form I-485 filing fee — paid online via USCIS Pay (confirmation No. [XXXXXX]) [Note: as of Oct 28, 2025, USCIS no longer accepts checks or money orders]
3. Form I-140 Approval Notice, dated [Date]
4. Priority Date confirmation / Visa Bulletin [Month Year] Chart A/B
5. Copy of passport biographical page (valid through [Date])
6. Copy of current visa stamp ([Type], valid through [Date]) [or "Visa exempt — see I-94"]
7. Copy of most recent Form I-94 (arrival/departure record)
8. Form I-693, Medical Examination and Vaccination Record (sealed envelope from civil surgeon [Name], dated [Date])
9. Two (2) passport-style photographs
10. [Employment letter or University appointment letter]
11. Personal Statement
12. [Any other items — sworn statement, NABC, translations, etc.]
```

For each derivative, create a separate numbered subsection.

**Closing**
"Please do not hesitate to contact [Applicant Name] / [Attorney Name, Bar No.] at [phone] or [email] if additional information is required. Thank you for your consideration."

### Critical Rules

- **Never guess the lockbox address** — it depends on the applicant's state. Flag as `[ACTION REQUIRED: verify lockbox address at uscis.gov/i-485]`
- **Receipt number must appear in the Re: line** — without it the officer cannot pull the underlying file
- **Fee note**: As of October 28, 2025, USCIS no longer accepts paper checks or money orders. Online payment only via pay.gov / USCIS Pay. State the payment confirmation number.
- **I-693 envelope must be sealed** — opening it invalidates the medical exam. Note this in the document list.
- **No originals** — USCIS retains submissions. Submit copies except where originals are explicitly required (photos, I-693 sealed envelope).
- **Biometrics fee**: Currently $85 for applicants 14–79. Include if applicable.

## employment-letter — Employment / University Appointment Letter

### Purpose

Verifies the applicant's current employment status, title, duties, and compensation for admissibility and adjustment purposes. Also used as evidence that the applicant will continue in the same field as their I-140 for NIW and EB-1 cases.

### Required Elements (in order)

**Letterhead**: Official organizational letterhead is required. Flag this clearly in the draft.

**Para 1 — Identity statement**
Full name, employer name, city/state, start date, employment type (full-time/part-time), and compensation with payment cadence (annual salary of $X, or hourly rate of $X).

Example: "Dr. Sarah Lin Chen has been employed full-time at Massachusetts General Hospital, Boston, Massachusetts, since January 15, 2024. She receives an annual salary of $118,000."

**Para 2 — Position and duties**
Job title, reporting structure, and a specific duty description. Specificity is required — especially for NIW cases where continued engagement in the national-interest field must be shown. Vague duties ("performs research") are insufficient.

For a researcher: "Dr. Chen serves as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Department of Biomedical Informatics. She is responsible for: (1) developing and validating machine learning models for early sepsis detection using multi-center ICU datasets; (2) collaborating with clinical staff to integrate predictive tools into the Epic EHR system; (3) presenting research findings at departmental grand rounds and national conferences; and (4) supervising two research assistants."

**For University Appointment Letters** (PhD students/postdocs):
Add: enrollment/appointment date, department, stipend amount and payment frequency, research topic description (2–3 sentences of real substance — "studying machine learning" is insufficient; "developing federated learning architectures that enable multi-hospital sepsis prediction without sharing patient data, addressing the HIPAA-compliance barrier to large-scale clinical AI validation" is sufficient).

**Closing**
Offer to provide additional information. Signed by an authorized individual (HR Director, Department Chair, PI — identify their authority). Original handwritten signature required.

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Letterhead present (flag if unknown)
- [ ] Compensation figure and cadence are explicit
- [ ] Duties are specific enough to establish field relevance
- [ ] For NIW: duties are in the same field as the I-140 endeavor
- [ ] Signer is identified and their authority is clear
- [ ] Date is spelled out (April 25, 2026 — not 4/25/26)

## personal-statement — I-485 Personal Statement

### Purpose

The applicant's own letter affirming (1) their intent to continue working in the same occupational field as their I-140, and (2) that this work remains in the national interest. Required for NIW and EB-1 cases where the basis is the applicant's own expertise. NOT a legal brief — no Dhanasar citations, no legal argument. Personal, direct, first-person.

### Required Structure

**Header**: Date. Addressed to "U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services" (not a named officer).

**Background section** (3–4 sentences):
State the applicant's field, degrees, and key accomplishments. Reference specific credentials — institutions, degrees, awards, major publications if applicable. This section establishes who the speaker is.

**Future Plans section** (4–6 sentences, must be specific):
Describe 2–3 concrete projects or goals the applicant intends to pursue in the US. Each one should answer: what is it, where will it happen (institution or context), and why does it matter to the US? Vague future statements are the most common weakness in personal statements.

Weak: "I plan to continue my research in AI and healthcare."
Strong: "Over the next two years, I will complete the validation of my sepsis prediction model across three hospital systems — Massachusetts General, Johns Hopkins, and UCSF — under IRB protocols already approved. I will then collaborate with Epic's clinical decision support team to integrate the model into ICU workflows. This deployment would make early sepsis intervention available at hospitals serving over 2 million patients annually."

**Closing** (2 sentences):
Express commitment to the US and willingness to answer questions. Sign with full name.

### Critical Notes

- First person throughout ("I," "my," "our")
- No legal citations — this is a personal statement, not a legal memo
- Length: approximately one page; maximum 1.5 pages
- Must be **signed by the applicant** — not the attorney
- The field described must match the I-140 petition exactly

## sworn-military-statement — Sworn Statement (Foreign Military Service)

### Purpose

Explains the nature and extent of foreign military service for I-485 disclosure. Required when the applicant served in a foreign military — USCIS must assess whether the service constitutes a ground of inadmissibility under INA § 212(a)(2)(E) (membership in a totalitarian party or persecutory force) or INA § 212(a)(10)(D) (draft evasion). Compulsory service in a non-persecutory military is generally not a bar.

This is a **notarized sworn statement**. The applicant must sign in front of a notary — never pre-sign.

### Required Elements

**Opening declaration**: "The following is a sworn statement regarding the nature and details of my military service in [Country]."

**Full name affirmation**: "My name is [Full Legal Name], and I hereby affirm that the information in this statement is true and accurate to the best of my knowledge."

**Nature of service**: Was it compulsory or voluntary? What age does the country require service? What were the specific duties (basic training, logistics, non-combat, combat)? Duration (exact dates if known, otherwise month/year)?

**Training location**: Name and location of the training facility or base.

**No persecutory conduct**: If applicable and true — state that the applicant did not participate in any persecution of civilians, did not commit human rights abuses, and was not a member of a unit known for such conduct.

**Closing declaration**: "I declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of the United States of America that the foregoing is true and correct." [Exact language required.]

**Signature line**: Signed, printed name, date — NOT SIGNED until before notary.

**Notarial block**: Include a blank notarial block — the notary will complete it.

### Format Note

This must be notarized. Draft must include explicit instruction: **DO NOT SIGN until in the presence of a commissioned notary public.** The notarial block is completed by the notary after witnessing the signature.
