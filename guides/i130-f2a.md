# Drafting Guide — I-130 Petition for Alien Relative (F2A Category)

**F2A** covers an LPR (Lawful Permanent Resident) petitioning for their **spouse** or **unmarried child under 21**. The central adjudication issue is proving the marriage is **bona fide** — entered in good faith, not for immigration purposes.

Governing law: INA § 204; INA § 216 (conditional residence); 8 CFR § 204.2. The fraud bar at INA § 204(c) permanently bars the beneficiary if USCIS finds the marriage was entered for immigration purposes.

**Key facts as of 2025–2026:**
- **F2A is currently current** (no priority date backlog for most nationalities) — verify at the monthly Visa Bulletin before filing.
- **Conditional residence (CR-1):** If married less than 2 years at the time of visa issuance, beneficiary receives a 2-year conditional green card. Must file I-751 to remove conditions within 90 days of the 2nd anniversary.
- **Concurrent NVC filing:** After USCIS approves I-130, the National Visa Center (NVC) processes the immigrant visa (if abroad) or the case is transferred to the appropriate field office (if adjusting status).
- **Fee change (Oct 28, 2025):** Same as all USCIS filings — online payment only via pay.gov / USCIS Pay. No checks or money orders.

## cover-letter — I-130 Cover Letter

### Purpose

Introduces the petition to the USCIS officer, establishes the petitioner-beneficiary relationship, and itemizes every enclosed document. USCIS will compare the document list against the package — completeness matters more than narrative quality.

### Required Structure

**Header block:**
```
[Date — month spelled out]

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services
[Correct lockbox address — flag as ACTION REQUIRED if unknown]

Re: Form I-130, Petition for Alien Relative
    Petitioner: [Full Legal Name], A Number: [if applicable]
    Beneficiary: [Full Legal Name]
    Relationship: [Spouse / Unmarried child under 21]
```

**Para 1 — Relationship and basis**
Identify petitioner's LPR status (date of LPR grant, A Number), name the beneficiary, state the relationship, and state the date and place the relationship began. For marriage petitions: "Petitioner [Name] is a Lawful Permanent Resident of the United States (A Number: [XXX], LPR since [Date]). [Name] and beneficiary [Name] were married on [Date] at [City, Country]. They file this I-130 to establish [Name]'s eligibility as the immediate relative spouse of an LPR under INA § 203(a)(2)(A)."

**Para 2 — Bona fide marriage statement**
One sentence affirming the marriage is genuine: "The marriage was entered in good faith and not for the purpose of obtaining an immigration benefit." Do not expand on this — the evidence documents carry the bona fide showing. Stating it in the cover letter is a signal to the officer that the package addresses the key adjudication issue.

**Document list** (numbered, exhaustive):
```
1. Form I-130, signed and dated [Date]
2. Form I-130 filing fee — paid via USCIS Pay (confirmation No. [XXXXXX])
3. Copy of Petitioner's Permanent Resident Card (front and back)
4. Marriage certificate — [Country], dated [Date] [with certified English translation if not in English]
5. Petitioner's birth certificate [with translation if applicable]
6. Beneficiary's birth certificate [with translation if applicable]
7. Passport biographical pages — Petitioner and Beneficiary
8. [If either party was previously married]: Divorce decree(s) / death certificate(s) establishing termination of prior marriage(s)
9. Joint bank account statements — [Bank Name], account [last 4], [date range]
10. Joint lease or mortgage — [Address], [date]
11. [Additional bona fide evidence: joint insurance, photos, communication records, etc.]
12. Affidavit(s) of [Affiant Name(s)] in support of bona fide marriage [count]
```

**Closing:** Standard offer to provide further information. Petitioner or attorney contact info.

### Critical Rules

- If either party was previously married, proof of termination of ALL prior marriages is mandatory — missing this triggers automatic rejection
- Translations: every non-English document must include a certified English translation (translator's certification of competence and accuracy, signed and dated)
- Photos: include a variety — engagement/wedding photos carry less weight than ordinary life photos (holidays, family events, home life) that show a shared domestic life
- Do not staple originals together; use a binder clip so USCIS can scan documents

## bona-fide-affidavit — Bona Fide Marriage Affidavit

### What This Letter Is and Is Not

This is a **sworn personal statement** from a third party — a friend, family member, or colleague — who has firsthand knowledge of the couple's relationship. It is NOT a character reference for either spouse individually. It is NOT a statement of support for the immigration petition. It is testimony that the affiant personally observed a genuine, ongoing marital relationship.

USCIS adjudicators are trained to identify form-letter affidavits written in legal language by people who've never met the couple. The affidavit must sound like a real person speaking from personal observation.

### Legal Purpose

USCIS uses these affidavits as corroborating evidence alongside documentary proof (joint lease, joint bank accounts, photos, insurance policies). A strong affidavit adds a dimension that documents cannot: the texture of daily life, the way the couple interacts, the social context of the relationship.

INA § 204(c) fraud bar: if USCIS finds the marriage was contracted for immigration purposes, the beneficiary is permanently barred from any future petition. Affiants who provide false statements can face perjury charges. The letter must be truthful — never draft content that overstates the affiant's knowledge.

### Required Elements (write in this order)

**Para 1 — Affiant identity**
Who the affiant is: full name, city/state of residence, occupation, and relationship to EACH spouse (not just one). How long have they known each person? Did they know them separately before they were a couple, or did they meet them as a couple?

Example: "My name is Maria Gonzalez. I am a licensed nurse practitioner living in Houston, Texas. I have known Sarah Kim for eleven years — we met as undergraduates at the University of Texas and have remained close friends. I met her husband, James Kim, shortly after they began dating in 2019, and I have come to know him well over the past five years."

Do NOT open with "I am writing this letter to support..." — that is a legal form opener, not a personal voice. Open with who you are.

**Para 2 — How the affiant knows the couple is a real couple**
Specific, dated events and observations. This paragraph is the core of the affidavit. USCIS looks for specificity that cannot be fabricated by someone who doesn't actually know the couple.

What to include:
- Named events attended with the couple (their wedding, holidays, birthdays, vacations — with approximate dates and locations)
- Observations of day-to-day life (visiting their home, seeing how they interact, knowing their routines)
- Conversations the affiant has had with each spouse about the other, about their shared life
- The couple's shared friends, extended family interactions, community involvement

What NOT to do:
- Generic: "They are very much in love and make a great couple."
- Unverifiable: "I can tell that their love is genuine."
- One-sided: "I've known Sarah for years and she is wonderful" — where's James?

Example of a strong specific paragraph: "James and Sarah got married on June 14, 2021, at a small ceremony in Austin, Texas, which I attended along with about thirty of their close friends and family. Since then, I have visited their apartment on Riverside Drive four or five times a year. I have celebrated Thanksgiving with them twice, joined them for James's birthday dinner last March at a restaurant downtown, and spent a weekend at a lake house in the Hill Country with them and two other couples last summer. What I observe when I'm with them is a couple that clearly shares a life — they finish each other's sentences about mundane things, argue about where to eat, and talk about their future plans together the way settled couples do."

**Para 3 — Any relevant additional observations**
If the couple has gone through hardship together (illness, job loss, long-distance period before one spouse's arrival), the affiant may speak to how they observed the couple managing it. If children are involved, mention them. If the affiant has met the couple's families together, mention it.

**Para 4 — Sworn declaration**
Must include: "I declare under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief." This exact language (or jurisdiction-equivalent) is required for the affidavit to be sworn. The affiant must sign in the presence of a notary.

Close with: "I am available to answer any questions USCIS may have regarding the information in this statement." Then signed name, typed name, date, address.

### Tone and Voice

Personal and direct. Written in the affiant's own voice — not legal language, not formal immigration terminology. A college friend writes differently than a work colleague; a parent writes differently than a neighbor. The voice must be consistent with who the affiant is.

The AI should calibrate based on the affiant's provided information: their relationship to the couple, how long they've known them, and their own background.

### Common Errors That Weaken Affidavits

- **Too short**: An affidavit under 300 words signals the affiant has little to say. Aim for 500–750 words of substantive content.
- **Legal language**: "Pursuant to my personal knowledge and belief..." signals a lawyer drafted it, not the affiant. USCIS discounts it.
- **No specifics**: "We have spent a lot of time together" — when? where? what happened?
- **Only knows one spouse**: The affiant must have observed the couple together, not just vouch for one partner's good character.
- **Notarization note**: Remind the user — the affiant must NOT sign the document until in front of a notary. The draft should include a notarial block placeholder.

### Format

- Length: 500–800 words of body text (not including the notarial block)
- No letterhead required — this is a personal statement
- Date the document
- Salutation: "To Whom It May Concern:" or "Dear Immigration Officer:"
- End with signed name, typed name, date, address, and a notarial block placeholder
- Output only the letter body — the platform adds headers

### Specificity Benchmark

The test for a strong affidavit: could it only have been written by someone who actually knows this couple? If the text could apply to any married couple, it fails. Each paragraph should contain at least one detail (a named place, a date, a specific event) that is verifiable by USCIS if they chose to follow up.

Benchmark paragraph (strong):
"I attended James and Sarah's wedding on June 14, 2021, at Barton Creek Resort in Austin, Texas — about thirty guests. Over the past three years I have visited their apartment on Riverside Drive at least four times a year, celebrated Thanksgiving there twice, and joined them on a long weekend trip to a lake house in Marble Falls last August with two other couples. Their domestic life is entirely ordinary: they share cooking, argue about temperature settings, and talk about renovating the bathroom in a way that only people who intend to stay together do."

### Evidence Pairing Note

The affidavit is corroborating evidence. In the cover letter, cross-reference the affidavit against documentary evidence. Example: "Affiant Gonzalez references the couple's Thanksgiving dinners — see photos Tab 7." Matching the affidavit's specific claims to photo or documentary evidence reinforces both.
